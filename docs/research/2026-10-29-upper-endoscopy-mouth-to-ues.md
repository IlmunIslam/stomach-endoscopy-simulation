# Upper endoscopy (EGD): mouth to upper esophageal sphincter (UES)

Date: 2026-10-29. Purpose: reference for the W0_Hypopharynx part. Nothing was downloaded.
Confidence tags: [S] = backed by a fetched or searched source (URL given), [K] = general clinical knowledge,
not verified against a source in this session (medium confidence), [U] = unverified or not found.

## 1. What the scope sees, in order

1. Mouth / oropharynx (scope over the tongue, guided by the bite block). View is looking back along the tongue; the
   tongue and base of tongue fill the TOP of the frame, the posterior pharyngeal wall is at the BOTTOM. [K, medium].
   Reason: the scope is introduced with the video "up" convention, so anterior structures appear up. Not source-confirmed.
2. Base of tongue and vallecula (the paired pockets between tongue base and epiglottis). [K]
3. Epiglottis: a pale-pink leaf, seen at the top-centre as the scope advances. [K]
4. Larynx opening (laryngeal inlet), beneath and behind the epiglottis. Sources list the vocal cords, inner epiglottis,
   corniculate cartilage, arytenoids and pyriform sinus as seen on pharyngeal passage. [S: PMC3678057 below]
5. Aryepiglottic folds (run from epiglottis to arytenoids and form the lateral rim of the inlet) and arytenoids
   (paired bumps at the back of the inlet). [K for fold detail; arytenoids S]
6. True vocal cords: paler, whitish-pearl bands inside the inlet, visible when the tip is angled down. [K for colour;
   cords visible S: StatPearls via search snippet]
7. Pyriform sinuses, left and right: two deep, collapsed, teardrop-shaped recesses either side of the larynx. The
   scope goes down the midline, behind the larynx, between them. [S for existence/ view; K for shape]
8. Postcricoid area: the mucosa of the posterior wall behind the cricoid, ending at the UES. In practice it is a
   closed, transverse fold/slit and the lumen is not open. [K]
9. UES (cricopharyngeus): a closed slit or collapsed ring at rest. Opens on swallow, on a burp (eructation), or when
   the endoscopist gently insufflates and pushes. [S for burp/visibility: PMC6945956 snippet: UES seen in 13 of 16
   subjects who could burp; K for swallow/insufflation]. Resistance is felt; the guidance is not to push hard. [S:
   Louisville slide title; pyriform perforation risk S: Springer chapter snippet]
10. Cervical esophagus: a round, pale-pink tube starting right after the UES. [K]

## 2. Look and feel

- Colour [K]: mucosa pink to red and wet; cords paler (white to pearl); epiglottis pale pink; pyriform sinus walls
  red-pink and moist. Secretions and saliva give bright specular highlights and small bubbles.
- Lighting [K]: the light source is at the scope tip, so brightness falls off strongly with distance (inverse-square
  look): near tissue is bright and nearly burnt out, the far lumen goes dark. Almost no ambient light.
- Lens [K]: wide-angle (about 140 degrees) fisheye-like barrel distortion; the edge of the frame stretches and
  curves, the circular image is cropped by a rounded mask on many processors.
- Speed [K, low confidence]: the passage from teeth to UES is brief, a few seconds. The endoscopist pauses briefly
  to identify the cords and pyriform sinuses, then holds at the UES for a swallow or burp and advances when it opens.
  I could not find a source giving real timings.
- First esophageal entry [K]: once through the UES the view changes to a collapsed, concentric pink tube with
  longitudinal folds, vessels visible, and the lumen opening with air.

## 4. Reference images and video to obtain (none are in references/ now)

Best types, in priority order:
1. Real transoral EGD intubation clips (mouth to UES to first 5 cm of esophagus), at least a few seconds each, to see
   scope speed, pauses, falloff and distortion.
2. Still frames, each landmark: vallecula, epiglottis with cords, aryepiglottic folds and arytenoids, left and right
   pyriform sinus, postcricoid closed slit, UES closed, UES open, first esophageal view.
3. Same landmarks from flexible laryngoscopy (nasal route, so the angle differs but the anatomy, colour and
   highlights match well).
4. Anatomy drawings or cadaver / 3D models for the shape of the pyriform sinus and cricopharyngeus (for modelling,
   not for appearance).
